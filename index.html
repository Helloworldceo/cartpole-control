How this works

Physics: the cart-pole is the standard nonlinear rigid-rod-on-cart model (frictionless pivot). State is [x, ẋ, θ, θ̇]; the only input is a horizontal force F on the cart. It's integrated with RK4 at a fixed timestep, sub-stepped independently of the animation frame rate.

PID: drives the pole angle to 0 with F = Kp·θ + Ki·∫θ + Kd·θ̇, plus a slow position-recovery term + Kx·x + Kdx·ẋ to stop the cart drifting off the track. Angle-only PID balances beautifully but leaves cart position uncontrolled — that coupling is exactly what makes this system harder than two independent single-loop problems, and exactly what LQR handles properly.

LQR: the nonlinear dynamics are linearized around the upright equilibrium numerically (central finite differences on the true nonlinear model — no hand-derived linear equations), giving matrices A, B. These are discretized at the simulation timestep and fed into the discrete-time algebraic Riccati equation, solved by iterating the backward Riccati recursion to convergence. The result is a single optimal gain vector K such that F = -K·(state − equilibrium) minimizes a cost trading off state error (weighted by Q) against control effort (weighted by R) — one unified law for all four state variables at once, recomputed live whenever Q, R, or the physical parameters change.

Try this: push the pole hard with PID position-centering gain at 0 — the pole recovers but the cart wanders. Then switch to LQR and push equally hard — position and angle recover together.
